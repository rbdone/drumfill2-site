A drummer in your phone.

The problems it was built for

Every musician without a drummer knows these.

DrumFill2 started from a short list of real situations: the solo act at a bar gig, the band rehearsing without its drummer, the learner looping a chorus, the songwriter who needs a drum track now. Here is each problem and what the app does about it.

A drum loop is not a song.

Loops never change, never fill, never end. DrumFill2 plays a full arrangement: Intro → A → B → Chorus (repeated) → Bridge → Outro, with fills into each section, a crash on the chorus and a real ending.

My hands are on the guitar.

In Follow chart it plays the whole form with zero input. In Follow me the keys are huge (64 px and up), fire the instant your finger touches, and nothing you need mid-song is behind a menu.

Follow chart vs Follow me →
The band stretched the solo. Now what?

Tap +2 BARS (as often as you like), LOOP to stay put, NEXT when the singer comes back in. Section changes always land on the bar line.

Stretch a section →
Machines drift. Screens go dark.

A sample-accurate engine keeps the time. Lock the phone, put it in your pocket, switch apps: the beat keeps going, with Play / Stop / Fill on the lock screen.

I don't have time to program drums.

Useful in 30 seconds: a featured groove is ready on first launch, plus 19 demo songs. Pick a genre and a form in Song Builder, type I2 V8 C8 V8 C8 O2 in Quick Chart, or beatbox it into the mic.

The gig is chaos.

Gig mode runs a pre-show check (Do Not Disturb, battery, output test, kits loaded) and locks the stage screen. Band view shows the band a huge beat counter. Setlists segue song to song.

It sounds like a toy.

Five multisampled acoustic kits recorded from real drums (velocity layers, round-robin), two synthesised electronic kits, your own SFZ or Hydrogen kits, and Freesound with your own free key.

Workflows

Six ways people use it.

Real screenshots from the app on an Android phone emulator. Swipe the pictures sideways.

Practice a song, then nail the chorus

  1. Library → a song. One tap opens it on Play, stopped, with the arrangement in the display.
  2. ▶ PLAY in Follow chart: a spoken count-in, then the whole form plays by itself and stops on its ending.
  3. Tap the Chorus block in the strip, then LOOP and TRAIN: the chorus repeats and speeds up 5 BPM every 2 passes.
  4. ■ STOP. A banner offers to save the new tempo to the song, or keep it just this time.
Practice guide →

Friday night: setlist, pedal, Follow me

  1. Setlists → New setlist, add your songs, and set what happens after each: Stop, Segue or Click through.
  2. 🎤 GIG MODE checks Do Not Disturb, battery, the output (with a test hit) and the kits, then START GIG opens Play with stage lock on.
  3. Switch to Follow me. Strum; tap FILL or NEXT between phrases, or press your pedal. END plays the outro.
  4. Between songs the big NEXT SONG key loads the next one. Turn the phone sideways for Band view.
Live guides →

From nothing to a full drum track in a minute

  1. Library → Build a song. Pick a genre (17 of them) and a form: Pop, Rock, AABA, 12-bar blues, Build/drop, Jam…
  2. BUILD SONG. Every section gets a groove, an end fill, an intro and an ending that belong together.
  3. Don't like a section? 🎲 Reroll it, or ⇄ Swap to browse grooves already filtered to the song's genre, feel and tempo.
  4. SAVE & PLAY. Fine-tune later in the Song Editor: cues, odd bars, tempo changes, ring-out endings.
Song Builder guide →

Type the chart the band just called

  1. Library → Type a chart. Type tempo, meter, genre and sections: @104 4/4 funk I2 V8 C8 (V8 C8)x2 Br8 C16 O2~
  2. The arrangement previews live as you type. A mistake gets a plain-English fix: “X4 isn't a section — did you mean S4 (solo)?” with a one-tap Use S4.
  3. The shortcut keys type the tricky bits for you: repeats, odd bars, cues, tempo changes, ring-outs.
  4. SAVE & PLAY. The builder fills in grooves for the genre, so it plays at once.
Quick Chart guide →

Beatbox the groove in your head

  1. Library → Beatbox. Headphones on for the click, choose 2, 4 or 8 bars, press ● RECORD and go “pm-ti-ta-ti”.
  2. The phone finds your kicks, snares and hats on a step grid and plays them back with a real kit, and says which library groove it sounds like.
  3. Or build a whole song: Song Builder → Build by beatboxing records each section in turn, with a 2-bar pre-roll into every new one.
  4. Teach it your sounds (5 of each) if your “ta” sounds like your “ti”. Recordings never leave the phone.

Make it sound like your band

  1. Kits lists the seven built-in kits with their licences; ▶ auditions each one.
  2. ↑ Import kit takes a Hydrogen .h2drumkit or a zipped SFZ kit; check the mapping, then IMPORT KIT.
  3. Browse free kits lists kits from a public feed with their licence badges.
  4. Get more sounds walks you through a free Freesound key in about two minutes, then lets you fill kit slots with CC0 (or CC-BY) one-shots, credited automatically.

Get DrumFill2 v1.0 for Android.

It isn't in an app store yet, so you install the APK file yourself (“sideloading”). It takes about a minute.

  • For 64-bit ARM phones (arm64-v8a): almost every Android phone from the last several years.
  • SHA-256 of the APK: 246be07d60afb479a6a28a078b7521598b56cfe5512fc024b78b706e181b633e
  • All checksums: SHA256SUMS.txt · v1.0.0 release page

Install it in four steps

  1. Open this page on your phone and tap the download button. If your browser warns about this file type, choose Download anyway.
  2. Open the downloaded APK (from the browser's downloads or the Files app). Android asks to allow installs from that app: tap Settings, switch on Allow from this source (“Install unknown apps”), then go back.
  3. Tap Install. If Google Play Protect says the developer is unknown, choose Install anyway. The APK is signed with the project's own key rather than a store key, which is why Android asks.
  4. Open DrumFill2 and tap ▶ PLAY. That's it: the featured groove plays with no setup.

Updating: install the newer APK over the old one. Your songs, setlists and settings stay. Uninstalling the app first would delete them.

Tip: headphones or a cable to the PA are best. Bluetooth speakers add a delay, so buttons feel late; the app warns you once and has a compensation setting.

From a computer with USB debugging on: adb install -r DrumFill2-v1.0.0-arm64-v8a.apk

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What does it cost?
The APK is a free download. There is no account, no sign-in, no ads and no server: everything runs on your phone.
Is there an iPhone version?
No. DrumFill2 v1.0 is Android only, installed from the APK above.
Why isn't it on Google Play?
Not yet. For now it's distributed as a signed APK. Updates are new APKs you install over the old one; your songs stay.
Do I need a foot pedal?
No. Every action has its own big key on the Play screen, and Follow chart needs no input at all. A pedal (Bluetooth MIDI, USB MIDI, or a page-turner that sends key presses) is an optional shortcut. See the pedal guide.
Does it need the internet?
No. Playing, building songs, kits and beatboxing all work offline. Only Freesound and the free-kit browser go online, and only when you use them.
Is Freesound included?
The app talks to Freesound directly with your own free Freesound API key, which stays on your phone. The setup wizard takes about two minutes. CC0 sounds by default; CC-BY optional; non-commercial sounds are never offered.
Where do the grooves come from?
The 300 patterns are original encodings of common, traditional grooves (backbeats, shuffles, bossa, one-drop…). None are transcribed from recordings. The sampled kits come from openly licensed recordings of real drum kits; Settings → Credits lists every author and licence.
Will it keep time with the screen off?
Yes. Playback runs in the background with a notification, and the lock screen shows Play, Stop and Fill. Unplugging headphones or a phone call stops it cleanly.
What about my beatbox recordings?
They stay on your phone and are only used to re-analyse a take. You can delete them in Settings → Beatbox recordings. Nothing is uploaded.
Which APK do I need?
DrumFill2-v1.0.0-arm64-v8a.apk, for nearly every modern phone. The v1.0.0 release page also has DrumFill2-v1.0.0-armeabi-v7a.apk for older 32-bit phones, DrumFill2-v1.0.0-x86_64.apk for x86_64 devices, and SHA256SUMS.txt with the checksums of all three.
